VOL. XX — 167 FRIDAY, FEBRUARY 24, 1967 PRICE: 10 PAISE

THE LAST RULER OF ASAFIA DYNASTY, HIS EXALTED HIGHNESS THE NIZAM, PASSES AWAY

NAMAZ-E-JANAZA AND STATE MOURNING DETAILS

The Namaz-e-Janaza of Nawab Mir Osman Ali Khan will be held tomorrow at the historic Masjid-e-Judi. The government has declared a state holiday, and all administrative offices, schools, and markets will remain closed. The national flag is ordered to fly at half-mast as the city prepares to bid farewell to its beloved leader. Security remains high around King Kothi as thousands arrive to pay their final respects to the soul of the departed ruler who served the people for over five decades with dedication and vision.

HAALAT-E-ZINDAGI (LIFE SKETCH)

Mir Osman Ali Khan was born in 1886 and ascended the throne in 1911. His era was defined by the establishment of the State Bank of Hyderabad and Osmania University. He was a pioneer in introducing modern administrative systems while preserving local heritage and secular values. His vision for a self-sufficient state led to the creation of the Osmania Rupee, making Hyderabad the only princely state with its own currency, postal system, and railway network. He worked tirelessly to integrate traditional culture with modern education. Under his rule, the city saw the birth of Osmania General Hospital, the High Court, and the State Central Library. As a visionary engineer in spirit, he built the Osman Sagar and Himayat Sagar dams to protect the city from devastating Musi floods. He donated millions to educational institutions like Aligarh and Banaras Universities. His architectural legacy transformed the skyline into a unique blend of Indo-Saracenic styles, and his social reforms ensured that every citizen had access to justice and opportunity regardless of their background. Despite being the world's richest man, the Nizam chose to live in a single room and wore simple cotton clothes. He was famous for his frugal habits but was incredibly generous during national crises. During World War II, his contributions were vital, yet he remained a man of the people, avoiding the luxuries his vast fortune could provide. He was often seen smoking local brands and spent his evenings in quiet contemplation or meeting with advisors to discuss the welfare of the poorest in his kingdom. His death marks the end of an era. The Deccan will forever remember him as the man who modernized Hyderabad. He leaves behind a legacy of tolerance and progress. Even in his final days, his concern was for the welfare of his subjects and the preservation of the cultural fabric of his beloved city. His funeral is expected to be attended by people from all walks of life, showing the deep respect and love the citizens held for their monarch who ruled with a soft heart and a firm hand for the state's progress.

THE FINAL RESTING PLACE

Masjid-e-Judi: Location for the funeral.

Arrangements at the mosque have been completed under royal estate supervision. The Nizam wished to be buried at Masjid-e-Judi, a place he frequented for prayers. The simple grave reflects the humility he maintained despite his status. Guards have been posted to maintain order as the city prepares for the massive procession. Thousands of workers have spent the day cleaning the area and setting up barriers to manage the expected crowd of mourners.

DOCTOR'S BULLETIN

The medical team confirmed that the Nizam had been suffering from influenza and chest congestion. His condition deteriorated rapidly at 11:00 PM yesterday due to respiratory complications. Despite emergency treatments, his vital signs weakened, and he passed away peacefully at 1:22 AM. Personal physicians Dr. Bankat Chandra and his team remained by his side throughout the final hours, providing all possible medical support.

Doctors noted his advanced age played a role in the body's inability to fight off the infection. His final moments were spent in the company of his closest family members and personal attendants in the familiar surroundings of King Kothi palace. A detailed medical history will be archived for official records as per the protocol of the royal house.

TAZIYATI

National leaders express sorrow. Prime Minister Indira Gandhi praised the Nizam's role in modernizing infrastructure. She noted that his demise marks the end of a legendary chapter in Indian history. The President and various state governors sent personal messages to the royal family.

The city is in a state of voluntary lockdown. Shops and schools closed immediately after radio bulletins. Businesses in the Pathergatti and Charminar areas halted activity as soon as the news arrived.

Foreign dignitaries from neighboring states are expected to arrive by evening for the state funeral. Special arrangements are being made at the airport to receive international guests and representatives of various royal houses who wish to pay their final respects.

Common citizens have gathered in groups at every street corner, sharing stories of the Nizam's kindness and the stability he brought to the state during his long reign.

H.E.H. Mir Osman Ali Khan (1886-1967)

PROCESSION

The Police Commissioner issued traffic guidelines for the Janaza procession route. Certain roads near King Kothi will be restricted to ensure the safe passage of the royal casket and attending dignitaries.

Public transport is at a standstill as citizens gather to discuss the legacy of the ruler. The route will be lined by thousands of volunteers to maintain peace and order throughout the ceremony.

Military honors will be presented during the final rites, recognizing his status as a former Rajpramukh and a distinguished historical figure.

CITY NEWS

The municipal corporation has activated emergency cleaning crews along the funeral route. Special water stations have been set up for the large crowds. The mood in the old city remains heavy with grief.

Osmania University students have organized a prayer meet. All academic activities have been suspended for three days to observe the mourning period.

Local radio stations are broadcasting tributes and playing sombre music in honor of the late Nizam throughout the day.

THE ROYAL FAMILY IN MOURNING

Prince Azam Jah, Prince Moazzam Jah, and other family members are coordinating with officials for final rites. Prince Azam Jah has been receiving visitors at the palace gates throughout the morning. The family has requested decorum and silence during the public procession to maintain the sanctity of the occasion. Tributes continue to arrive at the palace from all corners of the world, highlighting the global reach of the Nizam's influence and the deep ties of the Asaf Jahi house with numerous international organizations and nations.
